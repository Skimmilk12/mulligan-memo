Golf Tee Height Chart: Tee Length, Playing Height, and What the Makers Publish

A 3¼-inch tee is not 3¼ inches tall once it is in the ground. Every maker sells you the first number. One of them tells you the second. That gap is why so many people tee the driver wrong with the "right" tee.

The short answer

Tee length and playing height are different numbers. The rules cap a tee at 4 inches. Makers sell them at 1½, 2⅛, 2¾, 3¼ and 4 inches, and a 2¾ is what most of them call the standard driver tee. How much of that ends up above the ground is up to you, with one exception: Martini's Step-Up tee is built to sit the ball 1¾ inches off the turf. For a modern driver, Callaway's rule is that about half the ball shows above the crown, which works out to the bottom of the ball about 1.5 to 1.7 inches up. MyGolfSpy's testers gained about 14 yards going from a half-inch playing height to 1½ inches; Golf Monthly's tester kept gaining up to a 2¼-inch castle tee and lost distance at 2¾. Your own strike pattern picks the spot between.

Tee charts online tend to list tee lengths and call them heights. A tee has a length. The ball has a height above the ground. The difference between the two is however far you pushed it in, and nobody sells that.

The ChartWhat each maker publishes, and which number it is

Read the middle column. Six of the seven makers publish a length and nothing else. Martini publishes a ball height for its Step-Up. Brush-T publishes the depth its base sits at, which gets you closer, but its size is still the tee's size and not a measured ball height. With an ordinary tee, insertion depth determines playing height, and nobody publishes that.

The makers also disagree about what "standard" means. Evolve and 4 Yards More call 2¾ inches the standard driver tee. Pride says 2¾ is for drivers under 360cc, and puts today's 460cc heads on the 3¼ and 4. Martini builds its Original and Step-Up at 3¼. Same club, three different lengths, all "standard."

The RuleFour inches, and where that came from

The Equipment Rules cap a tee at 4 inches (101.6 mm), and separately forbid one designed or made so that it could indicate the line of play, unduly influence the movement of the ball, or otherwise assist the player in making a stroke. That is why the longest tee any maker sells is exactly 4 inches, and why 4 Yards More can market its green tee as "the longest allowed."

Four inches of tee is not four inches of height. Push a 4-inch tee an inch and a half into the ground and the ball sits at 2½ inches, which is already above the crown of any driver made.

The DriverHalf the ball above the crown, and what that is in inches

Callaway's own tip page puts it in one line: "About 1/2 the ball should be above the top of your driver as a general rule for tee height." Golf Digest and Golf.com say the same thing in slightly different words, and Zero Friction repeats it on its blog.

What that is in inches depends on the head. A Tom Wishon article on modern driver design, reproduced on Bob Sailer's clubmaking blog in 2014, gives a typical 450 to 460cc head a face height of 53 to 55 mm and a sole-to-crown height of 60 to 64 mm, which is 2.36 to 2.52 inches. A ball is at least 1.68 inches across, so half a ball is 0.84. Half the ball above that crown puts the bottom of the ball about 1.5 to 1.7 inches off the ground. Golf.com's Luke Kerr-Dineen, polling teachers, lands in the same place with "about 1.5 inches for driver," and it is what a 2¾-inch tee gives you when it is pushed in a little over an inch.

So the number to remember is the height of the bottom of the ball, not the length of the tee. For a 460cc driver and the half-ball rule, that is around 1.5 to 1.7 inches. A 2¾ tee pushed in an inch gets there. So does a 3¼ tee pushed in an inch and a half, and Martini's Step-Up sits a shade above it by design. A longer tee simply allows deeper insertion at the same playing height.

The TestsTwo published tests, two ranges

Two outlets have measured tee height with a launch monitor and human testers, and they looked at different ranges.

They agree that too low costs a lot. MyGolfSpy tested two heights and the higher won by about 14 yards; it did not look above 1.5 inches. Golf Monthly tested six sizes and the gains stopped between the 2¼ and 2¾ tees, when spin climbed and carry dropped. Two testers, different scales, small samples: not a rule. What they do show is that the top of the useful range is higher than most people tee it, and that there is a top.

Golf Digest's robot test, run at Golf Laboratories at 95 mph with a two-degree downward attack, looked at the other side of the same coin: strikes 0.6 inches below the centre of the face carried only 219 yards. A tee too low does not only lose height. It moves the strike down the face, and that is where most of the distance goes.

The Rest of the BagFairway woods, hybrids and irons

Here the instruction pages are the only source, and they are close but not identical. Murphy is a teacher; Ballengee and Kerr-Dineen are writers reporting teachers' advice.

The hybrid row is the one real split. Murphy tees it flush, Ballengee tees it like a fairway wood, and neither says why. The 1½-inch Pride, the 1¾ 4 Yards More and Evolve's 1.5 all exist for these rows. For an iron on a par 3, Murphy wants it to look as if it is not on a tee at all; Kerr-Dineen allows about a quarter inch.

FAQQuick answers

How high should I tee the ball for a driver?

So that about half the ball shows above the crown at address, which is Callaway's published rule and the one Golf.com and Golf Digest repeat. For a 460cc head that puts the bottom of the ball roughly 1.5 to 1.7 inches off the ground. Golf Monthly's tester kept gaining up to a 2¼-inch castle tee and lost distance at 2¾.

What length tee should I buy for a driver?

2¾ inches is what most makers call the standard, and it reaches the half-ball height when pushed in about an inch. Pride puts 460cc heads on its 3¼ and 4. Either works; a longer tee only allows deeper insertion at the same height.

What is the longest golf tee allowed?

4 inches (101.6 mm) under the Equipment Rules, which also forbid a tee that indicates the line of play, unduly influences the ball's movement, or otherwise assists the player. Pride and 4 Yards More both sell a 4-inch tee.

Which tees publish the playing height instead of the length?

Only Martini's Step-Up, which sits the ball 1¾ inches up when pushed to its bump. Brush-T publishes the depth its base sits at, 1.5 inches (1.75 for the XLT), but its sizes are the tee's sizes. Everyone else publishes length only.

How high should I tee a 3-wood or hybrid?

Fairway wood: barely off the ground, a bit less than half the ball above the crown. Hybrid: the sources split between flush to the grass and the same as a fairway wood. Iron on a par 3: barely on a tee at all, or about a quarter inch.

Does a higher tee give more distance?

Up to a point. MyGolfSpy's testers gained about 14 yards going from a half-inch to a 1.5-inch playing height. Golf Monthly's tester gained up to the 2¼-inch castle tee and lost distance at 2¾, when spin climbed. Golf Digest's robot showed why a low tee hurts: a strike 0.6 inches below face centre carried only 219 yards.
