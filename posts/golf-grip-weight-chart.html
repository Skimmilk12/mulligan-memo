Golf Grip Weight Chart: What Each Maker Publishes, and What a Regrip Does to Swing Weight

"Standard" is a size, not a weight. The makers' own spec sheets put a standard grip anywhere from 22 grams to 55, and they do not even agree on how much that changes your swing weight.

The short answer

A standard-size grip weighs 51.5 g as a Golf Pride CP2 or Tour Velvet on a 58 core, 48 g as a Winn Dri-Tac or a SuperStroke Traxion Tour, 42 g as a Winn Excel and 22 g as a Winn Dri-Tac Lite. Going up a size adds 1 g at Winn and 14 g on a Golf Pride MCC Plus4. A heavier grip lowers swing weight: Golf Pride says every 4 g is one swing weight point, Hireko says 5 g. So swapping a 51.5 g CP2 for a 48 g Dri-Tac raises each club by a little under one point. Put your own two grips in the calculator and it does the arithmetic under both rules.

Regrip a set and the only number anyone talks about is the size. The weight is on the spec sheet too, and it is the number that changes how the club feels in your hands, because it sits at the far end of the club from the head. Most people find this out after they have done all thirteen.

Lamkin's spec sheet could not be reached when this was checked and retailer figures are not the same thing, so Lamkin is not in the table. Three things in the table are worth a second look. First, the same grip in two colours can weigh differently: Golf Pride lists the single-colour MCC at 46.5 g and the dual-colour at 50, and the white Tour Wrap at 55 against 48 for the black. Second, "midsize" means almost nothing about weight. Winn adds one gram from standard to midsize. Golf Pride adds 3.5 g on a Tour Velvet and 14 on an MCC Plus4. SuperStroke's S-Tech adds 15. Third, Winn's Dri-Tac Lite at 22 g is less than half of anything Golf Pride makes, which is the whole point of it.

The DisagreementFour grams or five: the swing weight rule

Adding weight to the grip end lowers swing weight, because swing weight is a balance about a point 14 inches from the butt and the grip sits on the light side of it. Everyone agrees on the direction. The size of the effect is published two ways.

Four and five are not far apart, and on a small change they round to the same answer. On a big one they do not. Put an 82 g MCC Plus4 jumbo on a club that had a 50 g grip and Golf Pride's rule says you have lost eight swing weight points; Hireko's says six and a bit. Either way a D2 club is now somewhere around C6 to C8, which is why jumbo grips so often come with a request for lead tape on the head.

What "standard" weighs for the purpose of the rule also drifts. Hireko says a men's standard grip "tips the scales between 50 and 52 grams." Maltby's answer refers to a "standard 50-52 gram grip." A clubmaking manual in circulation uses 50 g for its table and "approximately 50-55 grams" in its text. So a 48 g Dri-Tac is a light grip by any of those baselines, and a 42 g Excel is lighter still, before you get anywhere near the 22 g Lite.

The Hidden GramTape

The grip is not the only thing you add. Maltby's answer puts two pieces of tape at "approximately 4.2 grams," which under his own rule is about one swing weight point before the grip has gone on. The clubmaking manual gives 2.8 g for a 10-inch strip of two-inch grip tape and 1.4 g for build-up tape, and says each 1/64 inch of build-up costs about half a point. If you are building grips up to a midsize with tape rather than buying midsize, the tape is doing some of the weight change on its own.

The ChoiceWhat to do with the number

If you liked how your clubs felt and are changing grips for feel or tack, match the weight. A CP2 to a CP2, a Dri-Tac to a Traxion Tour, a Tour Velvet to a Z-Grip, all within 2 g, and nothing changes but the rubber. If you are going bigger for comfort, our arthritic hands guide covers which oversize grips make sense; look at the midsize column first, because a Winn oversize costs you 2 g and a Golf Pride jumbo costs you 30. And if the set feels heavy in the hands after a regrip, the calculator above will tell you whether the grips did it before you start moving lead tape around.

FAQQuick answers

How much does a golf grip weigh?

A standard-size men's grip is usually 48 to 52 g: Golf Pride's CP2 and Tour Velvet are 51.5 g on a 58 core, Winn's Dri-Tac and SuperStroke's Traxion Tour are 48. Lightweight grips go much lower, Winn's Excel at 42 g and Dri-Tac Lite at 22. Jumbo grips reach 81 to 82 g at Golf Pride.

Does a heavier grip lower swing weight?

Yes. Weight at the grip end lowers swing weight. Golf Pride and Maltby say every 4 g is one swing weight point; Hireko says 5 g. A grip 8 g heavier takes a club from D2 to about D0.

Do midsize grips weigh more than standard?

Usually, but not by a fixed amount. Winn's Dri-Tac adds 1 g from standard to midsize, Golf Pride's Tour Velvet 3.5 g, its MCC Plus4 14 g, and SuperStroke's S-Tech 15 g. Check the maker's own figure for the model you are buying.

How much does grip tape weigh?

Maltby puts two pieces at about 4.2 g; a clubmaking manual gives 2.8 g for a 10-inch strip of two-inch tape and 1.4 g for build-up tape. Under the 4 g rule, ordinary grip tape is worth close to one swing weight point by itself.

What is the lightest golf grip?

Of the makers with published figures, Winn's Dri-Tac Lite at 22 g standard, 23 midsize and 33 oversize. JumboMax's UltraLite is described as up to 25 per cent lighter than a standard grip but JumboMax publishes no gram figure.

Does changing grip weight change how far I hit it?

Not directly. It changes the club's balance and how heavy the head feels, which can change your tempo. The makers publish the swing weight effect; none of them publishes a distance claim for grip weight.
