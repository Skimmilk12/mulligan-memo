The short answer

A standard men's steel 7-iron is 37″ and a standard women's steel 7-iron is 36″. Graphite runs half an inch longer than steel in the same iron, which most charts never mention. Whether standard is right for you depends less on your height than on your wrist-to-floor measurement — two golfers of identical height can need different lengths because of arm length and posture. The disagreementEven "standard" is not one number

GolfWorks puts a standard men's driver at 45.00″ with a heavier shaft, or 45.50″ with a lighter one. Callaway's pre-owned fitting guide states flatly that "the standard length of a driver is 45.75’’ for men and 44.75’’ for women."

That is up to three quarters of an inch of disagreement about what the word standard means, between two credible sources, on the club where length matters most. Neither is lying. A components supplier defines standard by swingweight; a manufacturer defines it by what it builds. But it does mean that "standard length" is a starting convention rather than a specification, and that buying a driver described as standard tells you less than you would think.

If it matters to you, measure the club rather than trusting the word.

The better inputWhy height alone gets it wrong

Nearly every chart online sizes clubs by height. Height is easy to know, which is why it wins — but it is not the measurement fitters actually use, and the people publishing the height charts say so themselves. Hireko's guide, which ranks at the top of this search, puts it plainly: "Height alone isn't perfect."

The reason is simple. Two golfers who are both six feet tall can have noticeably different arm lengths and stand differently over the ball. What determines how long the club needs to be is not how tall you are, but how far your hands are from the ground when you stand normally. That is exactly what wrist-to-floor measures.

So the calculator above takes both. When they agree, you get a confident answer. When they disagree — a tall golfer with long arms, a shorter golfer with short ones — it tells you they disagree and leans on the wrist-to-floor figure, because that is the one that describes your hands rather than your head.

The last wordWhat the number is for

A length chart cannot fit you. What it can do is tell you whether the clubs you already own are anywhere near right, and give you something specific to say when you do get fitted — "I'm playing standard steel irons and my wrist-to-floor is 33 inches" is a far better opening than "I think they might be a bit long."

And if you take one thing from the tables above, make it the graphite half-inch. A golfer who switches from steel to graphite in the same nominal iron has just added half an inch of club without changing a single number on the label.

FAQCommon questions

What is the standard length of a golf club?

It depends on the club, the shaft and whose standard you use. From The GolfWorks' published spec sheet, a men's steel 7-iron is 37″, a 5-iron 38″ and a pitching wedge 35.5″; graphite adds half an inch to each. Women's standard is a full inch shorter throughout. Drivers are less settled: GolfWorks lists 45.00″ with a 65g-plus shaft, while Callaway's fitting guide states 45.75″ for men — so "standard" is a convention rather than a fixed specification.

What size golf clubs do I need for my height?

As a rough guide, golfers between about 5'7″ and 6'0″ fit standard length, under 5'4″ typically goes about an inch shorter, and over 6'4″ about an inch longer. But height alone is a weak predictor — Hireko, who publish one of the best-known height charts, note that "height alone isn't perfect" because two golfers of the same height can have different arm lengths and posture. Measure wrist-to-floor as well and use both.

How do I measure wrist to floor for golf club fitting?

Stand up straight in the shoes you play in, on a hard flat surface, with your arms hanging relaxed at your sides. Have someone else measure from the crease of your wrist — where your hand meets your forearm — straight down to the floor. Do not stretch or hunch. That single number describes how far your hands sit from the ground, which is what club length actually has to accommodate, and it is why fitters use it in preference to height.

Are graphite golf clubs longer than steel?

Yes, by half an inch as standard, in every iron. The GolfWorks lists a men's steel 7-iron at 37″ and the graphite equivalent at 37.5″, and the same half-inch gap runs from the 2-iron to the lob wedge in both men's and women's sets. Graphite is lighter, so the club is built longer to arrive at a similar swingweight. It means switching shaft material without changing anything on the label changes the length you are swinging.

Why are women's golf clubs shorter than men's?

Standard women's clubs run one inch shorter than men's throughout the iron set — a men's steel 7-iron is 37″ against 36″ for women's — reflecting average differences in height and wrist-to-floor. It is a convention based on averages, not a rule about who should play what. A tall woman may well fit men's standard or longer, and a shorter man may fit what is sold as women's length. Measure rather than assume.

Does golf club length depend on the shaft weight?

For woods, yes. The GolfWorks splits its wood lengths by shaft weight rather than material: a men's driver is 45.00″ with a shaft of 65g or heavier and 45.50″ with one of 64g or lighter, and the same split runs through the fairway woods. Irons split by material instead, steel against graphite. It is an easy detail to miss, and it means two drivers described as standard can differ by half an inch.
