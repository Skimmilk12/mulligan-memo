Golf Shoe Size Chart: What Each Maker Publishes, and How to Switch Brands

There is no golf shoe size chart. Each maker publishes its own, and they put the same foot in different sizes. Here are five of them side by side, with the one brand that publishes a rule for switching.

The short answer

Measure your foot in centimetres, then read each maker's own table. A 28 cm foot is a US 10 at FootJoy, New Balance and SQAIRZ. It is a US 11 at adidas. At ECCO the first row that covers it is EU 44, which ECCO labels 10 / 10.5, because it builds whole EU sizes only. SQAIRZ publishes a rule for switching: keep your size if you come from FootJoy, Skechers, Puma or New Balance, and go a half size down if you come from adidas, Nike or ECCO. If a shoe feels tight, FootJoy's advice is to try a wider width before going up a size.

Golf shoes are sold in US sizes and made to centimetres, and the two are only loosely connected. Every maker publishes a table converting one to the other, and the tables disagree by up to a full size. That is not a rounding quirk. It is why the same golfer wears a 10 in one brand and an 11 in another and concludes that one of them "runs small."

Fit varies by model, but the tables vary by maker, and that is the part you can check. Once you have your foot length in centimetres you can read every one of them, and the rest of this page does exactly that.

The ChartFive makers, one foot

These are the makers' own numbers, men's, as published on their sizing pages in September 2026, read as the first row at or above each foot length. Where a maker prints inches as well as centimetres the centimetre figure is used. ECCO is shown in EU sizes because that is how ECCO builds; its chart labels each EU size with the two US sizes it is sold under.

Read across the 28 cm row. Three makers say 10. adidas says 11. ECCO's first row that covers 28 cm is the 28.3 cm row, EU 44, which its own chart labels "10 / 10.5". A golfer who has worn a FootJoy 10 for years and orders an adidas 10 is, by adidas's own table, ordering a shoe built for a shorter foot.

Nike, Puma, Skechers, Under Armour and G/FORE are not in the table for different reasons. Nike's chart is a general one, not a golf one. Puma's "JPN" column is a shoe size, not a foot length. Skechers publishes US, UK and EU only. Under Armour and G/FORE publish centimetre tables with half sizes, and G/FORE publishes two, a men's and a unisex, that put the same 28 cm foot at different men's sizes; they are covered in the text below rather than the table. Nike prints a warning worth keeping whichever chart you use: the centimetre size on a shoe box is not the same thing as your foot length in centimetres.

The RuleThe maker that publishes how to switch

SQAIRZ publishes a cross-reference to other brands as well as a centimetre table. Its rule is short enough to quote in full:

“If you wear FootJoy, Skechers, Puma, or New Balance, order your usual size. If you wear Adidas, Nike, or Ecco, order a half size down.”SQAIRZ, Sizing

Its table behind that rule puts a SQAIRZ 9 level with a FootJoy 9, a Puma 9, a Skechers 9 and a New Balance 9, and with an adidas 9.5, a Nike 9.5 and an ECCO 9.5. Set that against the centimetre charts and it only partly lines up: SQAIRZ puts its 9 at 27 cm, while adidas puts its 9.5 at 26.7 cm and its 10 at 27.1. And ECCO builds whole EU sizes and sells each under a pair of US labels, so "ECCO 9.5" means the EU 43 sold as 9 / 9.5, not a separate half size.

TRUE linkswear, which publishes no centimetre table on a static page, says the opposite thing about itself. Its shoes "run like athletic or running shoes, so a bit smaller compared to some big golf shoe brands like FootJoy, Ecco or Adidas," and it recommends sizing up if you are coming from FootJoy or ECCO. So the direction of travel depends on where you are coming from and where you are going, which is exactly why the centimetre is the only number worth carrying between brands.

The DisagreementBetween sizes: three answers

Land between two rows and the makers split three ways.

FootJoy's answer is the one aimed at the wide-footed golfer, and it assumes you can buy a width, which at FootJoy you can. New Balance, Under Armour and G/FORE sell widths on some models too, and adidas sells a set of golf shoes it labels Wide, but their between-sizes advice still says to go longer. A longer shoe does nothing for a wide foot except move the flex point away from your toes, which is the argument our wide feet guide is built on.

The WidthsWho actually sells them, and what the letters mean

Width letters are not standard across makers either. This is what each one publishes.

Note the naming. FootJoy's "X Wide" is a 3E. New Balance's "X-Wide" is a 4E. Skechers' "Wide" already reaches 3E, and its "Extra Wide" goes to 5E, which is wider than anything FootJoy sells. If you are shopping by the word on the box rather than the letter, you are not comparing the same shoe.

Women'sSeparate tables, same problem

FootJoy and ECCO publish separate women's length tables. New Balance does, and adds a cross-reference: a women's 9 is a men's 7.5, though its own women's table lists the 9 at 26 cm and its men's table the 7.5 at 25.5, so even one maker's two tables do not quite agree. adidas uses one chart for everyone and tells you a women's size is the men's size plus one. G/FORE runs unisex, with a men's 9 equal to a women's 10.5. Skechers does not make a women's Extra Wide. Measure in centimetres and the tables sort themselves out; shop by the number on your old shoes and you are back to guessing.

FAQQuick answers

Do golf shoes run small?

Not as a category. Makers publish different tables. adidas assigns a given foot length about one US size higher than FootJoy, New Balance and SQAIRZ do, so a FootJoy 10 is an adidas 11 by adidas's own chart. TRUE linkswear says its shoes run a bit smaller than FootJoy, ECCO or adidas and suggests sizing up.

Should I size up if a golf shoe feels tight?

FootJoy says no: try a wider width first, because going up a size is "the most common fit problem we see." New Balance, Under Armour and G/FORE tell you to choose the larger size when you fall between rows, and adidas says either, depending on whether you want a tight or a loose fit.

How do I convert my FootJoy size to ECCO?

ECCO sizes in whole EU sizes and pairs two US sizes to each one, so there is no exact answer. Its chart puts a 28 cm foot at EU 44, labelled 10 / 10.5. SQAIRZ's cross-reference treats ECCO as running a half size large against FootJoy. Measure in centimetres and use ECCO's own table.

What does 2E or 4E mean on a golf shoe?

Width letters. D is the standard men's width in most charts. 2E is wide, 4E is extra wide. But FootJoy calls a 3E "X Wide" while New Balance calls a 4E "X-Wide", and Skechers' "Wide" spans 2E to 3E. Compare the letter, not the word.

Is the cm size on the shoe box my foot length?

No. Nike states it directly: the centimetre size on a box or label is different from foot length in centimetres. Measure your foot and read the maker's table; do not read the box.

Which golf shoe brands sell wide widths?

FootJoy up to 3E, New Balance to 4E on some models, Skechers to 5E. Under Armour sells an "E" wide on some models and G/FORE an EE on some Gallivanters. adidas lists a Wide range without a letter width. ECCO, SQAIRZ, Puma and TRUE publish no width sizes.
