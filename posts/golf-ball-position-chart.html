Golf Ball Position Chart: Where Each Club Goes, and Why the Teachers Disagree

Every teacher puts the driver forward and the wedges in the middle. What happens to the eight clubs in between is where Nicklaus and Hogan part company with almost every chart on the internet.

The short answer

The chart most people want: driver just inside the lead heel, fairway woods and hybrids a ball inside that, long irons a ball or two forward of centre, mid irons on or just forward of centre, short irons and wedges in the centre. That is the per-club school, and Golflink, Performance Golf, MyGolfSpy and Golf Monthly all publish some version of it. The other school is Nicklaus, Hogan, Butch Harmon and Sean Foley: the ball never moves, it sits inside the lead heel or under your shirt logo for every club, and the trail foot moves instead. Both produce the same thing at impact. Pick one and stop adjusting.

Ball position charts look like they are reporting a fact. They are reporting a teaching method, and there are two. One moves the ball. One moves your feet. The people who moved their feet include the two best ball-strikers who ever lived, so it is worth knowing what they actually said before you print the chart.

The ChartPer-club positions, in ball widths from centre

A golf ball is 1.68 inches across, so "one ball width forward" is a real measurement and you can lay it out on the floor with a club shaft across your toes. The columns are four published charts. Where they disagree, the row says so.

Notice where the columns agree. The driver is forward, at or just inside the lead heel, in all four. The 7-iron is centre or a fraction ahead of it in all four. The fight is over the short irons and wedges, which two charts leave in the centre and one pushes an inch and a half back, and over how much the woods and long irons move, which ranges from "about an inch inside the heel" to "two ball widths forward of centre", a difference of about two inches for the same club.

The Other SchoolNicklaus, Hogan, Harmon, Foley: the ball does not move

Jack Nicklaus, in Golf Digest, with Jim Flick:

“I try to play every shot with the ball opposite a point just inside my left heel. That is a pure fundamental for me.” … “It might appear that I play the ball progressively farther back in my stance for fairway woods, irons and wedges, but that is more a result of my stance becoming narrower.”Jack Nicklaus, Golf Digest, March 2010

Hogan's Five Lessons puts every standard shot about half an inch inside the left heel and changes the stance instead: shoulder width for a 5-iron, wider for the long clubs, narrower for the short ones, with the right foot doing the moving. Butch Harmon, in 2015, said the same thing in fewer words: keep the ball in line with your shirt logo for every full shot, and "it's about how wide the right foot goes." Sean Foley's version is the argument for it. Changing ball position for every club "becomes guesswork as to where the ball should be played to hit it solidly and a reliable distance."

So the two great ball-strikers and two of the most-hired coaches of the last thirty years are all on the side the charts leave out.

The ReconciliationWhy both methods work, and what the launch monitor sees

A driver is hit on the way up and everything off the ground is hit on the way down. TrackMan's tour averages make the point in degrees: PGA Tour players hit the driver at an attack angle of about minus 0.9 and a 6-iron at minus 3.7, and LPGA players hit the driver at plus 2.8. TrackMan lists ball position first among the things that set attack angle.

The per-club school gets there by moving the ball toward the low point of the swing for the driver and back toward it for the irons. The constant school gets there by moving the trail foot, which shifts your centre, and with it the low point, relative to a ball that stays put. Brian Manzella, writing in Golf Digest in 2025, put it bluntly: Hogan "was one hundred percent tricking himself," because once the right foot had moved his ball position "would be pretty much like Tiger and Rory McIlroy." The ball was in a different place relative to his body. It just was not in a different place relative to his left heel.

Which means the chart above and the Nicklaus quote are the same instruction written in two coordinate systems. One measures from the centre of a stance that changes width. The other measures from a heel that does not move. If your stance gets wider for the driver and narrower for the wedge, a ball that "never moves" from your heel has moved a good two inches from the centre of your feet, which is roughly what the charts say it should.

The Missing ColumnStance width, which the charts assume and rarely state

The per-club charts only work with a stance that changes width, and most do not say by how much. The published figures that exist: Hogan, shoulder width for a 5-iron, wider for woods and long irons, narrower for the lofted clubs. Golf Monthly's Ben Emerson, shoulder width for a 7-iron and "one more step away from the target with your trail foot" for the driver. The USGTF is the only one to put numbers on it: the driver "near 2 inches outside of the shoulders for each foot," the 9-iron 2 inches narrower than each shoulder. On the same page it says the ball moves back 2.7 inches from driver to 9-iron for tour professionals. It cites no source for that, so treat it as a claim, not a measurement.

The ChoiceWhich one to use

If you can hit every club solidly from one spot, use one spot. Foley's consistency argument is the strongest thing on this page, and the players who did it are the proof. If your irons are fat and thin by turns, check the ball first, because the one thing every source agrees on is that amateurs play irons too far forward. Golfer Geeks says it outright. Move the 7-iron to the centre of your feet, hit ten, and see what the divots say. A divot that starts behind the ball says the ball is too far forward for where your swing bottoms out; a divot after the ball, and a ball that flies, says you have found it.

Either way, put a shaft on the ground across your toes and a second one pointing at the ball from between your feet. Ball position is invisible from where you stand over it. That is why the tour players use the sticks, and it is the only equipment this page needs.

FAQQuick answers

Where should the ball be for a driver?

Every published chart agrees: at or just inside the lead heel, roughly three ball widths forward of the centre of your stance. The driver is the one club hit on the upswing, and that is what puts it forward.

Where should the ball be for a 7-iron?

Centre of the stance or a fraction forward. Golflink says slightly ahead of centre, Performance Golf and Golf Leap say centre, Golfer Geeks says centre to an inch forward. Nobody puts it further forward than that.

Should the ball move for every club or stay in one place?

Both methods are taught by good teachers and both produce the same impact. Nicklaus, Hogan, Butch Harmon and Sean Foley keep the ball in one place and move the trail foot. Most published charts move the ball and keep the stance roughly the same. Pick one and use it for every shot.

Where do wedges go?

Middle of the stance in most charts; Performance Golf and Golfer Geeks allow up to a ball or an inch and a half back for full shots. The constant-position school keeps them under the shirt logo like everything else, with a narrow stance.

How wide should my stance be?

Shoulder width for a mid iron is the one figure every source shares. Wider for the driver, by "one more step" (Golf Monthly) or about two inches outside each shoulder (USGTF), and narrower for wedges.

Does ball position change attack angle?

Yes. TrackMan lists it first among the things that set attack angle. Tour players hit the driver near level or slightly up and the irons down, about minus 3.7 degrees for a 6-iron, and a forward ball meets the club later in the arc.
