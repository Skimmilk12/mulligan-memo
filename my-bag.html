Your Bag

My Bag

Your clubs, your numbers, kept in your own browser — and a check on the gaps between them, measured against what the makers actually publish.

How this works

Tap the clubs you carry and the lofts load from the maker's own published specs — or pick your iron set and get the whole thing at once. The bag then checks the gaps between every club, sorted by loft rather than by name, and tells you where the holes are. It saves on this device, so it will be here next time.

Your browser is blocking local storage. The bag will still work while this page is open, but it will not be here when you come back. Private or incognito windows usually cause this.

Step OneStart from a published set

Choose the irons you play and the lofts and lengths load from the maker's own spec table. If your set is not here, skip to step two and type the numbers in — they are usually on the maker's product page, and on many sets the loft is stamped on the sole.

or

Specifications are read from each maker's own published pages and checked August 2026. Loading a set replaces the clubs currently in your bag.

Step TwoYour clubs

Tap a club to add it. It arrives on a sensible number and you nudge from there — no typing unless you want to. The starting figure is not an invented standard: for irons it is the median of what the published sets on this site actually list, and for everything else it is a figure a maker publishes. Each club tells you where its number came from and how wide the real range is.

Step ThreeWhat your bag says

Add at least two clubs with lofts to see your gaps.

Gap guidance is measured, not invented: across the nine current iron sets on our loft chart, consecutive lofts step by 2° to 6°. Anything outside that range is wider or tighter than the makers space their own sets. Loft gaps are not distance gaps — the same four degrees opens a bigger yardage gap between long irons than between wedges.

Why botherBecause the label is not a measurement

A club number tells you where a club sits inside its own set and almost nothing else. A Titleist T350 7-iron is 29°; a Titleist 620 MB 7-iron is 35°. Both say 7. That is why this page asks for lofts rather than club names, and why it sorts your bag by loft when it checks the gaps.

The gap that catches most golfers is at the bottom of the set. Modern game-improvement irons finish with a strong pitching wedge — 43° is common — and a traditional 56° sand wedge sits thirteen degrees below it. Nothing on either club warns you, and no chart of standard lofts can tell you, because it depends entirely on which set you own.

The loft chart and putter checker read your bag directly. The rest have their own checkers that need their own numbers — a distance chart wants yardages, and the length and glove tools want body measurements, none of which live in your bag.
