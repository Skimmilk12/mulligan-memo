Your Bag

My Bag

Your clubs, your numbers, kept in your own browser. Build the bag once and every tool on this site works from your actual set instead of an average.

How this works

Pick your iron set and the lofts fill themselves in from the manufacturer's own published specifications. Add your wedges and woods, and the bag checks the gaps between every club you carry. Everything stays on this device. There is no account, nothing is uploaded, and no club data is sent to us or to anyone else — it lives in your browser's local storage and you can wipe it with one button.

Your browser is blocking local storage. The bag will still work while this page is open, but it will not be here when you come back. Private or incognito windows usually cause this.

Step OneStart from a published set

Choose the irons you play and the lofts and lengths load from the maker's own spec table. If your set is not here, skip to step two and type the numbers in — they are usually on the maker's product page, and on many sets the loft is stamped on the sole.

or

Specifications are read from each maker's own published pages and checked August 2026. Loading a set replaces the clubs currently in your bag.

Step TwoYour clubs

Add anything else you carry — driver, woods, hybrids, wedges, putter. Loft is what the bag works from, so fill that in wherever you can; length is optional and only used to compare against the published standard.

Step ThreeWhat your bag says

Add at least two clubs with lofts to see your gaps.

Gap guidance is measured, not invented: across the nine current iron sets on our loft chart, consecutive lofts step by 2° to 6°. Anything outside that range is wider or tighter than the makers space their own sets. Loft gaps are not distance gaps — the same four degrees opens a bigger yardage gap between long irons than between wedges.

Why botherBecause the label is not a measurement

A club number tells you where a club sits inside its own set and almost nothing else. A Titleist T350 7-iron is 29°; a Titleist 620 MB 7-iron is 35°. Both say 7. That is why this page asks for lofts rather than club names, and why it sorts your bag by loft when it checks the gaps.

The gap that catches most golfers is at the bottom of the set. Modern game-improvement irons finish with a strong pitching wedge — 43° is common — and a traditional 56° sand wedge sits thirteen degrees below it. Nothing on either club warns you, and no chart of standard lofts can tell you, because it depends entirely on which set you own.

Once your bag is here, these pages read from it:

  • Loft chart & gap checker — where your set sits against what the makers publish
  • Distance chart — your yardages, with your real lofts beside them
  • Club length chart — your lengths against the published standard
  • Glove size finder and putter length checker — the rest of the fit

PrivacyWhere this data lives

In your browser, on this device, and nowhere else. My Bag uses local storage, which is a small amount of space a website is allowed to keep on your own machine. We cannot read it, it is never transmitted, and it does not appear in any analytics we collect — the only thing measurement ever sees is that the tool was used and how many clubs were counted, never the clubs themselves.

That also means the bag is tied to this browser. Open the site on your phone and you will start with an empty bag. Clearing your browser data clears it too. If that matters to you, print the spec sheet and keep the paper.
